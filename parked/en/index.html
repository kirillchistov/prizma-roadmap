Working name · founding blueprint · 10 Nov 2026

    Executive recommendation

    Start as a decision-led growth operator for established consumer brands. Earn the platform from the work.

    Do not launch as a full-service agency and do not launch as self-serve SaaS. Launch a productized, managed service that owns one recurring decision for mid-sized consumer brands: where the next euro of marketing budget should go, proven in contribution margin, with every change approved, logged and evaluated. Build the software only where the service repeats.

    Ffounder context Ssourced fact Hhypothesis Aassumption Rrecommendation Qopen question

    The five calls

    1. RBeachhead: established, mid-sized consumer brands selling direct online and on marketplaces, spending roughly €30k–€300k a month on paid media across 2+ channels (bands to validate).
    2. RFirst use case: a monthly “profit per euro” budget decision cycle: diagnose, reallocate within guardrails, run one causal test per quarter, report in contribution margin.
    3. RModel: managed service on an internal platform, sold as a fixed fee banded by spend under management, not as a percentage of media.
    4. RDefer enterprise CPG as a primary market for 12–18 months; re-enter through retail-media and promotion measurement once the measurement core is proven.
    5. RAutonomy: recommendations by default; autonomous execution only for reversible, pre-budgeted actions inside a written policy each client signs.

    Why this shape

    • SPlatforms are automating execution itself. Meta has publicly targeted end-to-end automated campaign creation by end-2026[9], so competing on campaign operation is a shrinking moat.
    • SHolding groups now sell integrated AI platforms (Omnicom Omni after the IPG deal, WPP Open, Publicis CoreAI)[6][7][8], and WPP has an SME tier, Open Pro[8]. Head-on “AI agency” positioning lands in a crowded field.
    • HWhat stays scarce for mid-sized brands is independent judgement about incrementality and margin, plus the discipline to turn it into approved actions. That is service-heavy at first and productizable later.
    • FThe founders already plan a scenario-forecasting tool for SMEs. It becomes the diagnosis and planning module rather than a separate bet.
    Business goal→Data-readiness & diagnosis→Scenario plan→Approved budget & brief→Activation→Sales & margin→Causal test→Decision log→Next plan

    02 · Customer needs and segment selection

    Three segments, one classification rule, one beachhead

    Definitions before rankings

    “Largest CPG market”

    RRank countries by FMCG retail sales value, all channels, in constant USD at fixed exchange rates, covering food, beverages, personal care and home care. This is the measure Euromonitor uses for its global figure of about USD 6.6 trillion in 2025[1].

    QThe country-level top-10 list sits in a paid Euromonitor report[2], so no ranking is given here. Buy that table, or use a proxy: household final consumption on food, non-alcoholic beverages and household/personal goods from national accounts. Do not substitute ad-spend rankings.

    “Top-five local brand”

    RA brand ranked in the top five by retail value share within one category in one country, whose brand owner is headquartered in that country and is not one of the 50 largest global FMCG companies.

    Keep four entities separate in the CRM: brand (what is advertised), brand owner (who signs), category, country. A company like a global food group is one owner with many brands across many category-country cells; it is one account with several possible buyers.

    Classification rule (one segment per account)

    1. Enterprise CPG if the brand owner sells mainly through third-party retail and either is a global top-50 FMCG company or owns a top-five local brand in a top-10 market.
    2. Else High-growth if company revenue grew ≥40% in the last 12 months and its category grew faster than the national FMCG or sector average and it shows product–market-fit evidence (repeat purchase or retention cohorts).
    3. Else Mid-sized established if it has ≥3 years of trading, positive gross margin, an existing repeat-customer base and the eligibility criteria below.

    Size, category growth, product maturity and marketing need are stored as separate attributes; the segment label is derived from them in this order, so no account is counted twice. Thresholds are Aassumptions to tune after 30 accounts are scored.

    Segment detail: jobs, requests vs needs, triggers, disqualifiers, interview questions

    Enterprise CPG

    Expressed request: “AI for faster content”, “a retail-media strategy”.
    Underlying need: a defensible, independent read on which mix of media, trade and retail media grows category share, and faster localized execution.

    Buying triggers: annual planning, agency review, a retailer’s new media network, category share loss.

    Data reality: Schannels vary sharply by country; online channels are about 40% of FMCG sales in China and South Korea but most other APAC markets are offline-led[3]. Consumer-level data is mostly absent; retailer data access is negotiated.

    Disqualifiers: exclusive holding-group measurement contract; no sell-out access; procurement cycle longer than 9 months for a pilot.

    Interview questions: Walk me through how this year’s retail-media budget was set. Which number did finance challenge? What would make you trust an outside test over your network’s report?

    Evidence to observe: a budget line under review; access to weekly sell-out by region; willingness to fund a paid test.

    Mid-sized established

    Expressed request: “more sales”, “a better agency”, “lower CAC”.
    Underlying need: confidence and time. Someone accountable who connects spend to profit and explains what to approve next.

    Buying triggers: margin squeeze, a bad quarter after raising spend, incumbent agency renewal, new marketing hire.

    Data reality: e-commerce and ad data usually complete; margin per SKU often sits in finance spreadsheets.

    Disqualifiers: spend below the band; no clean order history; expectation of guaranteed revenue; refusal to pause any spend for testing.

    Interview questions: Tell me about the last time you changed the marketing budget. What did you look at? How did you know it worked? Who disagreed?

    Evidence to observe: names a doubted decision; can export data within a week; asks about price.

    High-growth (discovery)

    Expressed request: “growth hacking”, “scale fast”.
    Underlying need: a disciplined experiment system that protects unit economics.

    Candidate verticals to compare: functional food and beverage D2C, protein and GLP-1-adjacent nutrition[18]; vertical SaaS with self-serve funnels; subscription health and wellness services; premium pet care; refurbished electronics.

    Out of scope: tactics that breach platform policies, fake reviews or engagement, dark patterns, unsupported health claims, and growth with negative contribution after returns and discounts.

    Interview questions: What is your payback period by channel? Which test last changed your plan? What would you never do to grow?

    Evidence to observe: cohort retention data; a growth lead with authority; tolerance for null results.

    Attractiveness, weighted

    Scores 1–5 per criterion (higher is better for us, so burden and competition are inverted). Move the weights or pick a founder profile. HScores are judgements to replace with discovery evidence.

    03 · Beachhead and positioning

    Own the budget decision for established direct-selling consumer brands

    Beachhead ICP R

    EU/UK consumer brands, €5M–€100M revenue, selling through their own e-commerce plus at most one or two marketplaces, €30k–€300k monthly paid media on 2+ channels, ≥18 months of order data, a marketing lead without a marketing-science function, and a CEO or CFO who doubts the reported return. Categories that overlap CPG (food, beverage, personal care, home) are preferred: they build the capabilities the CPG segment later needs.

    Initial use case

    Monthly profit-per-euro cycle: one shared goal (contribution margin at a target growth rate), a reallocation proposal across channels with confidence ranges, an approval inbox, execution inside guardrails, and one pre-registered incrementality test per quarter that updates the model.

    Customer promise

    “Every month you will know what your marketing spend returned in margin, what we will change next and why, and what needs your approval. Every major change is tested or explained.”

    We are responsible for

    Diagnosis, measurement design, budget recommendations, executing approved changes in agreed channels, creative briefs and variants within scope, reporting. We are not responsible for total company growth, pricing, product, stock levels, retailer negotiations or sales-team performance; those appear in reports as stated constraints and confounders.

    Why this
    The decision recurs monthly, has a visible money consequence, needs data the client already has, and is hard to do well without statistics skills that mid-sized brands rarely employ.
    Why now
    SStrong open-source MMM and geo-testing libraries exist (Meridian, Robyn, PyMC-Marketing, GeoLift)[14][15], but practitioners report that data quality and judgement remain the binding constraint[14]. Platform automation also concentrates targeting decisions inside each platform, raising the value of an independent cross-channel view.
    Rejected alternative
    Full-funnel agency for enterprise CPG first: highest willingness to pay, but long procurement, entrenched holding-group platforms[6][7] and poor data access make it slow to learn.
    What must be true
    Enough clients pass the data gate; buyers value independence enough to pay a fixed fee; delivery hours fall with each cycle.
    What would change it
    A CPG design partner willing to fund a retail-media measurement pilot within 90 days, or evidence that mid-market buyers only pay for execution volume, not decisions.

    CPG-specific requirements (for later entry)

    • Sell-in vs sell-out: shipments to retailers distort weekly sales; model on sell-out (retailer EPOS or panel) and treat sell-in as an inventory signal.
    • Confounders to model explicitly: distribution (weighted numeric distribution), price index vs category, promotion depth and frequency, out-of-stocks, feature/display.
    • Retail media: SWARC forecasts USD 200.4bn of retail media spend in 2026 and USD 223.4bn in 2027, with growth outside Amazon slowing to 9.8% in 2027[4]. Network-reported ROAS needs independent geo or store-level validation.
    • Brand time horizon: long-term effects need longer adstock and brand-health tracking; short tests understate them.
    • Dependencies: retailers and distributors control data, shelf and price; the platform records these as external constraints, never as our levers.

    Platform core vs segment workflows vs custom work

    Repeatable core

    Connectors, canonical metrics, margin model, readiness gates, MMM/test engine, approval inbox, policy engine, decision log, weekly brief.

    Segment workflows

    D2C: cohort and payback. CPG: sell-out, promo and retail media. High-growth: experiment backlog and payback guardrails.

    Custom (priced separately or refused)

    Bespoke integrations, brand campaigns, PR, one-off research. Rule: any custom task repeated for a third client becomes a core or segment feature, or is dropped.

    Avoiding the consultancy trap: track delivery hours per workflow per client each month; automate the top two hour-consumers each quarter; cap custom work at 15% of delivery hours; standard SOW with fixed scope; decline clients outside the ICP even when they would pay.

    Three journeys

    Regional brand lead, enterprise CPG

    1. Receives quarterly retail-media test proposal from her brand team champion.
    2. Reviews design: regions, duration, spend at risk, decision rule; approves with legal note.
    3. Sees a weekly status line, not a dashboard.
    4. Gets a verdict with interval and a finance-ready memo; exports to her planning tool.

    Needs: governance, exports, single sign-on, no new tool to learn.

    Owner of a mid-sized brand

    1. Monday: phone notification, “3 decisions need you, 2 minutes”.
    2. Opens inbox: “Move €6k from search brand terms to prospecting video. Expected +€2–5k margin. Reversible within 1 day.” Approves.
    3. Month-end: one page with margin from marketing, what changed, what was learned.

    Needs: plain language, money units, mobile, trust.

    Growth lead, high-growth company

    1. Works in the experiment board daily; adds hypotheses.
    2. Inspects power analysis, raw data and model diagnostics.
    3. Sets guardrails (max CAC, payback) that let the system run tests autonomously.

    Needs: depth, speed, APIs and exports, more autonomy.

    06 · Measurement, decision science, autonomy and governance

    Measure at the level the data can support, and say so

    Five numbers that are not the same thing. Correlation (spend and sales moved together) · attributed conversions (a platform or model assigned credit) · modelled contribution (MMM estimate, assumption-dependent) · experimental lift (randomized or quasi-experimental difference) · incremental profit (lift × margin − media − fees − discount and return costs). Reports label every figure with which one it is.

    Measurement ladder by data maturity

    TierEntry conditionMethods usedNot usedDecisions it can support
    L0 Reporting<12 months data or tracking breaksFinancial reporting, contribution margin, cohort basics, qualitative evidenceMMM, fine-grained attributionFix tracking; protect margin; small reversible tests
    L1 Diagnostic12–18 months, 2+ channelsPlatform attribution as diagnostic, pre/post with synthetic control, platform lift studiesMMM as a decision toolCreative and audience choices within channel; flag suspicious ROAS
    L2 Causal≥18 months; geo-level sales; ≥10–20 comparable regionsGeo experiments, holdouts, switchback testsMMM without calibrationChannel-level budget shifts
    L3 Integrated≥2 years weekly data, spend variation, several completed testsBayesian MMM calibrated with test results, forecasting and scenario optimizationUncalibrated extrapolation beyond observed spendAnnual and quarterly budget allocation across channels

    Data sufficiency and failure modes

    • SGeo tests: practitioner guidance suggests planning a minimum detectable effect of 5–10% over 4–8 weeks, with a cooldown after[15]; quasi-experiments want roughly 25+ pre-treatment periods and 10–20+ geo units, ideally a year of history[15].
    • SMMM can mislead badly under default settings: one published synthetic test reported a modelled return of 10.61 for a channel whose true return was 4.20[14]. Calibrate with experiments and check against known effects.
    • Confounders: promotions, price, stockouts, seasonality, competitor activity, PR, distribution changes, platform algorithm shifts, tracking changes.
    • Small brands: when power is too low, do not pretend. Use larger, longer, reversible changes; Bayesian priors from the library; and state the decision as a bet with a review date.

    09 · Grounding material and next actions

    Founder thesis and working documents

    One-page founder thesis

    Problem. Mid-sized consumer brands spend tens to hundreds of thousands a month on marketing without a trustworthy answer to what that spend returns in margin. Platform reports grade their own work, agencies are paid on volume, and statistics skills are scarce.

    Insight. As platforms automate execution, the scarce asset becomes independent, causal judgement turned into accountable decisions.

    Solution. A managed decision cycle on our own platform: readiness gate, margin baseline, scenario plan, approval inbox, guarded execution, pre-registered tests, decision log.

    Business. Fixed fees banded by spend; no media margin. Service first, software as delivery hours fall; licence the measurement core to independent agencies in year 2; enter CPG through retail-media measurement.

    Moat. A growing library of calibrated effects by category and channel, each client’s decision history, and a clean governance record.

    Proof required in 90 days. Two paid pilots, one acted-on reallocation each, delivery hours trending toward 40.
